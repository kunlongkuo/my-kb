# VideoExpress v3.5 — Create Prompts grammar and the 10 animation styles

**Language:** every example in this file is the official English original, kept verbatim for its structure. The notebook's output is always 繁體中文 — imitate the 繁中 template and worked example in `02-modes.md §0`, not the English wording here.

Source: the official VideoExpress v3.5 reference "Create Prompts · 10 Animation Styles" (received 2026-09-19). It contains no prose rules, only one Text-To-Image example and one Image-To-Video example per style. The grammar below is what those ten examples share; the per-style table is what differs between them. The HTML app encodes the same data in `MEDIATYPES` and `i2vSkeleton()`.

**One non-official addition:** `shadow-puppet` (traditional Chinese shadow-puppet theatre) is not in the official reference. Its §2 row and §3 example come from this distribution's own VideoExpress runs (2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09) and are marked as such; its example is in 繁體中文 because that is the wording that was tested.

## §1 The eight-tag Image-To-Video grammar

Every official I2V prompt is **one line** made of eight literal uppercase bracket tags, always in this order:

```
[REFERENCE USE] [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] [SCENE] [ACTION 0-3s] [CAMERA] [LIGHT AND IMAGE] [PRODUCTION SOUND] [NEGATIVES]
```

| Tag | What goes inside | Fixed wording worth reusing |
|---|---|---|
| `[REFERENCE USE]` | Declares the uploaded image as the exact opening frame and what it controls | "Use the supplied image as the exact opening frame; it controls the composition, the world, the lighting and the exact framing of this shot, and the location, palette and camera come from it." Nine of the ten official examples open with "Use the supplied image as the exact opening frame…"; only the 3D one numbers it "Picture 1". Numbering is kept for first-last, which has two images (Picture 1 opening, Picture 2 closing). |
| `[IDENTITY / CONTINUITY]` | Who is in frame (Actor Alias + continuity anchor), then the continuity lock | "Exactly one continuous shot, no cuts. Keep every character's face, hair, skin tone and wardrobe identical to the opening frame for the whole shot." |
| `[SCENE]` | Framing, subject, setting as staged in the frame, light direction and colour, plus one line for the dramatic beat | "The dramatic beat of this shot: …" |
| `[ACTION a-bs]` | ONE small subject motion per segment, then hold. Official examples use a single `[ACTION 0-3s]`; multi-beat shots use consecutive contiguous segments (`[ACTION 0-2s] … [ACTION 2-5s] …`) that cover the full duration. Dialogue lives here as a speech-act wrap (`01-core.md §3`) | "…then holds still. His body, hands and feet remain in their original positions." |
| `[CAMERA]` | ONE move only | "One move only: slow steady push in. Subject motion and camera motion are separate; hold the rest of the frame steady." |
| `[LIGHT AND IMAGE]` | Continue the frame's lighting and grade, then the style's material lock (table below) | "Continue the exact lighting, colour grade and <style look> of the opening frame." |
| `[PRODUCTION SOUND]` | Ambience only | "Natural scene ambience matched to the location, present and continuous, no music, no narration, no added speech." (speaking shots: "the only speech is the quoted line") |
| `[NEGATIVES]` | Style-specific material failures first, then the common list | "Avoid: distorted faces, warped mouths, extra limbs, morphing, flicker, watermarks, logos, slow motion, frozen frames." (the official list also has on-screen text, captions, subtitles: dropped, see below) |

Rules the examples imply:

- Never drop, rename, translate or reorder a tag. In 繁體中文 output the tags stay English.
- One continuous shot, no cuts, one camera move, one motion per `[ACTION]` segment. Exception: a commercial, music-video or action montage written as one plain timed paragraph with cuts inside it (`styles/live.md` "Fast-cut montage and camera moves", field-tested 2026-11-04); it does not use the eight tags. The dogfood rules about closing micro-actions, the 2 words/second budget and no trailing "Animate natural lipsync…" sentence still apply inside the tags.
- No "Animate this image into a N-second…" prefix. `[REFERENCE USE]` is the opener.
- In a shot with no people write `Characters present: none` and drop the face and anatomy items (`distorted faces, warped mouths, extra limbs`) from `[NEGATIVES]`; naming faces in an empty scene invites the model to add one (`01-core.md` photoreal faceLock lesson).
- Fill every slot. The emitted line contains no angle brackets, and the character reference is the literal Actor Alias (`Actor 1`), never a descriptive name.
  - Anti-pattern ❌: `[IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] Characters present: <Mole Gardener>, <continuity anchor>.`
  - Correct ✅: `[IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] Characters present: Actor 1, the stout charcoal-clay mole gardener with brass spectacles and a cream apron.`
- **Settled 2026-11-19 (open since 2026-09-19):** every official example lists `on-screen text, captions, subtitles` inside `[NEGATIVES]`, which contradicts `01-core.md §10`. On a 2026-11-19 film the speaking shots whose [NEGATIVES] listed 畫面文字、字幕 burned captions into the frame and the re-rolls without them came out clean, so those words are dropped; watermarks and logos stay. The official examples below keep their original wording; the 繁中 output never copies those words.
- Every official example is a 3-second shot. This distribution keeps its 5-9s per-shot planning (`02-modes.md §1.2a`) until a real run shows otherwise.

## §2 Per-style table

`mediaType` is the key used by the HTML app and by `04-platform-videoexpress.md` Recommended defaults. `t2iBase` opens every T2I prompt; `[LIGHT AND IMAGE]` and `[NEGATIVES]` are the two I2V fields that change per style.

| mediaType | Style | T2I opener (`t2iBase`) | `[LIGHT AND IMAGE]` continues… | Style-specific `[NEGATIVES]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| `3d` | 3D Animation | Cinematic high-end 3D animated feature film still | Pixar-grade 3D animation of the opening frame. Photographic depth, no plastic flatness | plastic flatness, rubbery skin, waxy doll faces |
| `claymation` | Claymation | Beautifully crafted claymation stop-motion film still | handmade clay texture, miniature-set depth and restrained stop-motion cadence of the opening frame. Materials stay tactile and stable | glossy plastic surfaces, smooth CGI skin, sliding feet, melting or re-sculpting clay |
| `pixel-art` | 8-Bit Pixel | Detailed retro pixel-art cinematic scene | crisp pixel-art rendering of the opening frame. Coherent square pixel clusters and stepped edges hold through the movement, no smoothing or texture crawling | anti-aliased smoothing, morphing pixels, texture crawling, photorealism, readable lettering |
| `stop-motion` | Stop-Motion | Cinematic photograph of a handcrafted stop-motion miniature set | fixed practical lighting, shallow miniature depth of field and imperfect handmade surfaces of the opening frame. Gentle stop-motion cadence without distorting anatomy | glossy plastic, disappearing strings or joints, smooth CGI motion, lighting changes, camera shake |
| `comic-book` | Comic Book | Cinematic graphic-novel illustration | bold ink contours, fine hatching, textured shading and printed palette of the opening frame. Reflections stay subtle and tied to surfaces | lettering, speech balloons, saturation shifts, photorealistic conversion, softened or vanishing ink lines |
| `watercolor` | Watercolor | Detailed watercolor-and-ink storybook illustration | transparent washes, paper grain and muted pigments of the opening frame. Painted details remain stable rather than boiling or crawling | boiling or crawling paint, photorealistic textures, 3D rendering, warped limbs |
| `wool-felt` | Wool | Cinematic handmade wool-and-felt miniature scene | tactile wool fibers, visible stitching and soft miniature depth of field of the opening frame. Small, deliberate movement with subtle stop-motion timing | melting or smoothing fibers, changing stitches, a moving mouth on a stitched face, exaggerated motion |
| `paper-cut` | Paper Cut | Cinematic layered paper-cut stop-motion film still | matte paper fibers, layered cut edges and soft miniature shadows of the opening frame. Restrained, slightly stepped stop-motion movement appropriate to a paper puppet | realistic skin, glossy plastic, rippling or bending cardboard, fluttering paper, blurred cut edges |
| `ghibli` | Ghibli | Cinematic hand-drawn Japanese animated film still in the style of Studio Ghibli | delicate outlines, cel shading and painted background textures of the opening frame. Painted textures stay stable throughout | photorealism, 3D rendering, extra limbs, detached hands, line boiling, background repainting |
| `low-poly` | Low-Poly | Cinematic low-poly 3D animation still | faceted planes, flat shading and edge lighting of the opening frame. Geometry stays angular through the movement | texture smoothing, subdivision into smooth surfaces, transformation into clay or live action, face drift |
| `shadow-puppet` | Shadow Puppet (non-official, field-tested) | Traditional Chinese shadow-puppet theatre stage still | warm back-lit glow, translucent dyed leather and crisp openwork carving of the opening frame. The leather pieces stay rigid and flat against the screen and pivot only at their rivet joints, in stepped settle-and-hold beats | realistic skin, rounded 3D puppets, bending or folding leather, a bowing or hunching torso, a puppet turning toward the camera, blurred carved edges, broken or extra control rods |

T2I traits shared by all ten official examples: "widescreen 16:9" stated up front, one full paragraph of subject (face, hair, wardrobe, pose, hands), one paragraph of environment with left/right placement, one paragraph of light + material + mood, and a closing "No text or watermark." Since 2026-09-20 this distribution's T2I Shape (`02-modes.md §1.2a`) follows that four-movement form at 150-250 words; length is welcome, subject count is not (`04-platform-videoexpress.md` T2I composition complexity guidance).

## §3 The ten official examples (verbatim, few-shot reference)

Use these as pattern references, not as content to copy. Each pair is one Text-To-Image prompt and the Image-To-Video prompt written for the image it produces.

### 3D Animation (`3d`)

**Text-To-Image**

> Cinematic high-end 3D animated feature film still, widescreen 16:9. A young girl adventurer stands on a grassy coastal cliff at blue-hour dusk, centered in a waist-up portrait. Her shoulders angle slightly while her face looks toward the viewer, with a gentle closed-mouth smile and quietly hopeful brown eyes. She has a rounded youthful face, large expressive eyes, thick dark eyebrows, a small nose, rosy cheeks, and delicate freckles.

> Jet-black hair falls in loose side-swept bangs, with a thick braid hanging over her right shoulder on the left side of the image. Fine loose strands blow dramatically toward the right. Oversized vintage aviator goggles sit fully visible on top of her head, with weathered brown leather padding, tarnished brass rims, and dark blue reflective lenses. A thick burnt-orange knitted scarf wraps around her neck, its long fringed end streaming across the right side of the frame. She wears a worn brown coat with broad taupe lapels, dark buttons, conspicuous hand-stitched rectangular patches on the chest and upper sleeve, and a muted olive vest underneath.

> Below her on the left is a picturesque seaside village of closely packed stone cottages, steep slate roofs, chimneys, and warm glowing windows. The ocean extends into the distant left horizon. Behind her on the upper right, a rugged grass-topped rocky headland supports a tall lighthouse with an illuminated amber lantern and a small keeper’s cottage. Layered dark blue-gray storm clouds fill the sky.

> Soft warm light illuminates her face from the front left, balanced by cool coastal twilight and subtle rim light. Detailed knitted fibers, natural skin shading, carefully groomed hair, weathered leather, soft cinematic shadows, atmospheric haze, and shallow depth of field. Crisp focus on her face with the village and lighthouse softly blurred but recognizable. Rich, polished stylized 3D rendering, wistful adventurous mood. No text or watermark.

**Image-To-Video**

> [REFERENCE USE] Picture 1 is the opening frame - it controls the composition, the world, the lighting and the exact framing of this shot. Take the location, palette and camera from it. [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] nila owns this frame. Characters present: Nila, a determined 12-year-old girl with a single black braid, brass goggles pushed up on her forehead, a long orange knit scarf and a patched brown work coat. Exactly one continuous shot, no cuts. Keep every character's face, hair, skin tone and wardrobe identical to the opening frame for the whole shot. [SCENE] Low-angle close-up of Nila on the ridge, brass goggles pushed up, jaw set and eyes fixed on the sky, wind pulling her orange scarf taut across her shoulder, the dark town roofs soft and far below her. Cold blue dusk light from the left with a faint copper rim on her cheek.. The dramatic beat of this shot: Nila has until midnight to give it back its fire. [ACTION 0-3s] slow steady push in on her face as her scarf whips past the lens [CAMERA] One move only: slow steady push in. Subject motion and camera motion are separate; hold the rest of the frame steady. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] Continue the exact lighting, colour grade and 3D Pixar animation of the opening frame. Photographic depth, no plastic flatness. [PRODUCTION SOUND] Production sound: natural scene ambience matched to the location, present and continuous, no music, no narration, no added speech - the narrator voiceover is laid over this clip separately. [NEGATIVES] Avoid: distorted faces, warped mouths, extra limbs, morphing, flicker, on-screen text, captions, watermarks, subtitles, logos, slow motion, frozen frames.

### Claymation (`claymation`)

**Text-To-Image**

> A beautifully crafted claymation stop-motion film still, widescreen 16:9, depicting a friendly anthropomorphic mole tending his flowers inside a rustic miniature greenhouse. Medium-wide eye-level composition. The stout, rounded mole stands full length slightly left of center, turned three-quarters toward the right. He has charcoal-gray sculpted clay skin with softly stippled texture, a long rounded snout, a prominent pink oval nose, small warm brown eyes, and a gentle pleased smile. Tiny circular brass spectacles perch high on his snout. He wears a grubby cream gardening apron fastened with two large brown buttons, with a wooden-handled metal trowel tucked into its front pocket. His left hand hangs at his side with long pale digging claws, while his raised right hand holds a soft off-white cleaning cloth beside the greenhouse wall. Broad clawed feet rest on the reddish brick floor.

> A rough wooden planting bench stretches across the right half of the image, supporting individual weathered terracotta pots of tulips in muted purple, dusty pink, coral, and yellow. Above the flowers, four handmade prize rosettes hang in a horizontal row on the timber wall: purple, rust red, faded blue, and mustard yellow, each with a pale circular center and two ribbon tails. A battered gray metal watering can occupies the lower-left foreground. Additional plant pots, stacked empty terracotta containers, and leafy seedlings fill the shelves on the left.

> Aged timber framing, dusty translucent glass panes, and sloping greenhouse roof beams surround the scene; a soft rural landscape appears through the opening behind the mole. Warm diffused afternoon light, subdued earthy colors, tactile clay fingerprints, handmade fabric texture, imperfect miniature props, gentle shadows, and shallow cinematic depth of field. Keep the mole, cloth, flowers, and rosettes clearly readable. Cozy, patient, affectionate storytelling atmosphere. No text, logos, or watermark.

**Image-To-Video**

> [REFERENCE USE] Use the supplied image as the exact opening frame for a three-second continuous claymation shot. [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] Preserve the mole’s charcoal clay body, pink nose, brass spectacles, gentle expression, cream apron, pocket trowel, claws, and proportions. Maintain the raised hand holding the cloth, the bench of tulips, four rosettes in their existing order, watering can, pots, greenhouse framing, and background. [SCENE] The same warm, rustic miniature greenhouse, with dusty glass, aged timber, terracotta pots, and a reddish brick floor. Retain visible handmade clay texture and miniature-set craftsmanship. [ACTION 0-3s] The mole makes one short, gentle downward wiping motion with the cloth against the wooden wall immediately beside the purple rosette, then settles his hand. His feet and hanging arm remain planted and relaxed. Keep the cloth in contact with his fingers and avoid shifting the ribbons or flowers. [CAMERA] One slow, subtle push toward the mole and the row of rosettes, preserving the eye-level three-quarter composition. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] Consistent warm diffused afternoon light, soft shadows, muted earthy colors, shallow depth of field, and a restrained stop-motion cadence. Materials stay tactile and stable. [PRODUCTION SOUND] Soft cloth brushing rough wood and very faint greenhouse ambience; no speech or music. [NEGATIVES] No cuts, additional gestures, new props, moving flowers, changing rosette colors, facial distortion, extra claws, sliding feet, glossy plastic surfaces, text, or logos.

### 8-Bit Pixel (`pixel-art`)

**Text-To-Image**

> Detailed retro pixel-art cinematic scene, wide landscape composition approximately 2:1, evoking a richly illustrated classic adventure game. A small boy stands with his back to the viewer on a city sidewalk, looking through the glass entrance door of a warmly glowing neighborhood arcade. Place him slightly left of center, full body, wearing a bright red baseball cap backward, a short-sleeved shirt with blue, white, and yellow horizontal stripes, blue jeans, and red-and-white sneakers. His dark hair peeks beneath the cap and his arms hang at his sides.

> The arcade occupies the center and right two-thirds of the composition: weathered dark brick and concrete facade, a broad red striped awning, a glass door on the left, and a large display window on the right. Five vintage arcade cabinets stand in a row inside, each with a glowing screen and a small round stool. Golden-orange ceiling lights illuminate the interior. Above the awning is a wide dark rectangular marquee framed by a dense border of bright orange bulbs, with mottled pixel highlights and no legible lettering.

> On the left, a curved black streetlamp glows above a parked blue-gray boxy car. Beyond the waterfront, silhouetted city buildings and a suspension bridge recede into a spectacular orange sunset with purple and rust-colored pixel clouds. Add sidewalk cracks, small weeds, metal rubbish bins, and a low planter beside the doorway.

> Use clearly visible square pixels, crisp stepped silhouettes, clustered pixel shading, selective dithering, and intricate pixel-level detail throughout. Strong contrast between cool indigo shadows and saturated amber light spilling across the pavement. Eye-level street view, nostalgic, peaceful, inviting evening atmosphere. Entire image rendered as cohesive pixel art; no smooth photographic surfaces, vector gradients, interface, watermark, or readable text.

**Image-To-Video**

> [REFERENCE USE] Use the supplied pixel-art arcade image as the exact first frame and composition reference. [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] Preserve the boy's red cap, striped shirt, blue jeans, red sneakers, rear-facing pose, and scale. Keep the arcade cabinets, awning, marquee, streetlamp, parked car, bridge, and skyline in their original positions. [SCENE] A quiet waterfront neighborhood arcade at orange sunset, rendered entirely in crisp, richly detailed retro pixel art. [ACTION 0-3s] The boy makes one small, curious head tilt toward the arcade window, then holds still. His feet and hands remain in place. Everything else stays structurally stable. [CAMERA] One very slow straight push toward the boy and doorway, with minimal movement and no cuts. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] Preserve warm amber interior light, orange sunset, indigo shadows, and bright marquee bulbs. Maintain coherent square pixel clusters and stepped edges during the movement, with no smoothing or texture crawling. [PRODUCTION SOUND] Quiet arcade electronics and a low, distant city ambience; no intelligible speech and no music. [NEGATIVES] No walking, opening doors, new people, moving vehicles, cabinet rearrangement, readable text, morphing pixels, flicker, photorealism, camera shake, scene changes, or watermark.

### Stop-Motion (`stop-motion`)

**Text-To-Image**

> Cinematic photograph of a handcrafted stop-motion miniature theater, widescreen 16:9. A small wooden prince marionette stands alone slightly left of center on an old wooden stage, shown head to toe with generous space around him. His body faces the camera and his head turns very slightly toward the right. He wears a small irregular antique gold crown, a faded crimson velvet knee-length royal coat with round brass buttons and broad cuffs, a cream shirt, a narrow dark belt, and short brown trousers. His exposed wooden lower legs and carved shoes stand close together. Articulated joints are visible at the elbows, wrists, and knees. His hands hang loosely by his sides.

> Give him a delicately carved pale wood face with visible grain, small black painted eyes, thin brows, a long simple nose, faded rosy cheeks, short sculpted brown hair, and a melancholy, uncertain expression. Several thin marionette strings descend visibly from the top of the frame to his head and limbs. His clothes have miniature stitching, worn velvet pile, and softly frayed edges; his wooden surfaces have tiny chips and handmade imperfections.

> Deep burgundy theater curtains frame both sides, with a large gathered curtain and gold tassel trim on the right. Behind him, a softly focused painted theatrical backdrop depicts a shadowy lake, trees, distant mountains, and a medieval castle on a rocky hill in the upper right. Include a small ornamental planter near the backdrop.

> A single warm amber spotlight from above illuminates the puppet and creates an oval pool of golden light across the worn stage planks, with a long soft-edged shadow stretching toward the lower left. Dark surrounding stage, muted brown and gold palette, subtle atmospheric dust, shallow depth of field, tactile practical miniature materials, intimate storybook mood, authentic puppet-scale photography. No text or watermark.

**Image-To-Video**

> [REFERENCE USE] Use the supplied miniature theater image as the exact first frame. Preserve the original staging and handcrafted stop-motion appearance. [IDENTITY / CONTINUITY] Keep the same wooden prince marionette, crown, red velvet coat, cream shirt, carved face, exposed joints, and visible strings. Preserve his position left of center and the surrounding curtains, wooden floor, painted lake, and distant castle backdrop. [SCENE] A dim puppet theater under a warm amber spotlight, with tactile miniature fabric, aged wood, and subdued brown shadows. [ACTION 0-3s] The marionette makes one slight, hesitant head turn toward the right and then pauses. His body and feet remain planted; the attached strings respond with a barely perceptible tension change. [CAMERA] One restrained, slow forward push toward the puppet, retaining his full body in frame throughout. No cut or orbit. [LIGHT AND IMAGE] Maintain the fixed spotlight, golden pool on the stage, soft floor shadow, shallow miniature depth of field, visible wood grain, and imperfect handmade surfaces. Use gentle stop-motion cadence without distorting anatomy. [PRODUCTION SOUND] Faint wooden joint creak and quiet theater room tone; no dialogue, narration, applause, or music. [NEGATIVES] No walking, dancing, new gestures, disappearing strings, moving backdrop, curtain motion, facial morphing, extra limbs, glossy plastic, lighting changes, camera shake, text, or watermark.

#### Field notes — non-official, tested 2026-10-09 (Chinese period drama as wooden marionettes)

Not part of the official reference. Tested in 繁體中文 on VideoExpress with two reference-locked characters (Consistent Character), three shots × two takes: every take stayed a carved wooden puppet with the same face and wardrobe, including a cool-light flashback shot that turned into live action under the non-official `shadow-puppet` style. Facing or turning toward the camera is not a failure here, unlike flat shadow puppets.

- Generator settings: Creative mode on, Image Type Photorealistic (cinematic), Enhance off, for the character masters and every first frame.
- Character master: one marionette, full body and small in frame ("從頭頂到鞋尖完整出現在畫面中，木偶只佔畫面高度約七成，上下留白"), plain dark backdrop, no props. With Creative on the masters came out without strings; the shot first frames added visible strings on their own, so no fix was needed.
  - Use a turnaround master when the character has a feature seen only from the side or back (a queue, a bun, something worn on the back) and the storyboard has side or back shots. Wording: "手工停格動畫微縮場景的角色設定照片，16:9 寬螢幕。同一尊木偶並排出現三次，三個全身視圖等距排成一列、彼此不重疊：左邊是正面，中間是臉朝畫面右的純側面，右邊是背面。三個視圖是同一尊木偶、高度相同…" then the usual puppet description, "背面視圖清楚看到腦後那條長辮", and "無文字、無標籤、無編號、無格線、無水印". Tested 2026-10-11: the sheet came out clean on the first try (three matching views, no labels). Against a front-facing single master, on a pure-profile shot and a back three-quarter shot, six first frames each: the queue rendered as a braid in 12 of 12 against 5 of 12 (the single master hid the queue, so the model guessed: loose ponytails, long loose hair). One puppet and the asked angle in 12 of 12 for both; no sheet layout leaked into a frame. It does not change the motion: turning to the camera and extra gestures stayed the same.
  - For a character already in use, draw the turnaround from its existing master: the sheet prompt as a shot with `Cast: Actor N` and `Save: master` (ve-runner saves the picked frame as a new master, no video). Keep the wardrobe and hair wording; for the face write only "臉完全照參考圖，同一張光滑的淡色木雕臉、同樣的五官與表情，不加皺紋或刻紋". Tested 2026-10-11, six sheets each: with the original face wording ("四十歲上下", "眉心一道直紋") the faces aged and gained carved wrinkles, about 1 of 6 close to the master; with the reference-only face line about 5 of 6 close. Still check the sheet for a bald crown or changed shoes. Used as the master on the back three-quarter shot, the queue came out braided in 5 of 8 first frames: better than the old single master (2 of 6) but below a freshly drawn sheet (6 of 6), so check the queue at QC.
  - A back three-quarter shot whose `[ACTION]` turns the head a little further turned the whole body to face the camera in 4 of 4 takes (both masters). Keep back shots to motions that do not turn the head.
- Setting: name the miniature set and its few surfaces ("清末人家簡樸廚房的微縮場景：灰白色的土牆、一扇木格窗、舊木板地面"). One candidate still added a hanging lamp and extra crockery; pick it out at QC.
  - Text alone does not hold a room across shots: with the same room sentence in every prompt, the room and bed changed from shot to shot (0 of 12 first frames matched one reference room), and the bed came out as a modern mattress or the sleeper lay on the floor. Before the shots, draw an empty set master for each recurring location: a shot with `Cast: none` and `Save: master`, a wide view of the whole room, "房間裡沒有任何人、沒有任何木偶", and every piece of furniture the scene uses placed by side of frame (which wall, left or right half), with its structure spelled out ("木製架子床：四根直立的床柱撐著頂架，床板離地約一尺、下方看得到床腳"). Show it to the user before the shots.
  - In single-character shots put the set master in Reference Photo 2 (character in slot 1). ve-runner only fills slots from actors, so add the room as an `Actor N:` portrait line, cast it (`Cast: Actor 1, Actor 2`) and pass both library ids with `--masters`. The shot prompt needs no new wording. Tested 2026-10-12 (a Qing bedroom, four shots × three first frames each, identical prompts, slot 2 empty vs set master): room, bed, mosquito net, window, door and desk matched the set master in 12 of 12 against 0 of 12.
  - The set master also locks the camera angle. A reverse shot (camera at the mirror, bed on the right) came out at the set master's own angle in 3 of 3, and that shot had the most duplicates (two copies of the character or an extra person in 2 of 3, against 1 of 3 without it). Draw one set master per camera direction the scene uses (the room seen from the door, the room seen from the bed) and cast the one that matches the shot. Count the people in every first frame at QC.
  - Two-character shots fill both slots, so this does not apply to them; not yet tested.
- Props drawn as pictures: write "水墨人像" rather than "剪影"; a silhouette next to puppets invites more puppets.
- Remaining failure: too much motion. A head-lift shot walked in both takes and a raised-hand shot spread both arms, despite "走動、新的手勢" in `[NEGATIVES]`. Plan two or three takes per shot and pick one.
  - Give each `[ACTION]` its speed and its duration, and give the hold its own seconds: "Actor 1 用整整三秒，非常緩慢、均勻地抬起頭，望向畫面右側的木格窗。" then "頭停在抬起的位置，之後兩秒保持站立不動。" instead of "Actor 1 抬起頭…" then "之後保持站立不動。" Tested 2026-10-11, one standing head-lift shot, three takes each on the same first frame: walking 1 of 3 against 2 of 3, turning to face the camera 1 of 3 against 3 of 3, and the speed takes kept the three-quarter profile. Small sample, one action; still pick from takes.
- Keep the strings. A full 11-shot run (three takes each) kept every take a puppet, but strings sometimes broke or drifted; a no-string variant ("木雕人偶", no strings named anywhere) removed that glitch but moved far more: both seated takes stood up and walked off, both standing takes walked toward the camera. The visible strings seem to hold the puppet in place. Pick around string glitches at QC instead.
- Describe the light, never its source: "一盞暖琥珀色的燈從畫面左上方打下" drew a hanging lamp into both first-frame candidates; "暖琥珀色的光從畫面左上方斜斜照進來" did not.
- Keep posture out of the puppet description: "像停格動畫裡自己站著、自己坐著的木偶" made a standing shot come out seated.

With Creative off, a shot that uses a character reference turns into rounded 3D puppets in a realistic room; with the 2D Image Type plus Enhance the puppets come out as 3D cartoon dolls.
- Character master: one puppet, full body, small in frame ("從頭頂到腳底完整入鏡，只佔畫面高度約七成，上下留白"), plain screen, no props. Without that wording Creative mode crops at the waist.
- Pure profile is forced by "只看得到一隻眼睛". Rivet joints and the wrist rods must be named or the figure is drawn as one solid piece.
- Describe the light, never its source: "被後方油燈照透" drew an oil lamp or a bulb into two of three shots.
- A hat is drawn only when its shape is given ("六片拼成的半球形便帽，帽頂一顆紅色小結，帽下沒有髮髻").
- Stepped motion is finished in post: resample to 8 fps and back to 24 fps (`fps=8,fps=24`); 12 fps is not enough. When several clips are joined, cut each segment by cumulative frame count (`trim=end_frame=`), not by seconds: per-segment rounding to 1/8 s drifted 0.22 s over 11 segments.
